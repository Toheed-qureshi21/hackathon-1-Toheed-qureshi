# RedeemTrip — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Product Overview

**Product Name:** RedeemTrip  
**Product Type:** AI-assisted flight search and travel rewards optimization platform  
**Document Status:** MVP specification  
**Primary Goal:** Help users identify suitable flights and understand whether redeeming reward points offers better value than paying cash.

RedeemTrip lets users describe their travel plans through a conversational interface. The application gathers trip requirements and rewards information, searches for available cash fares and award flights where supported, compares the available options, and explains the results using verified data and deterministic calculations.

The product is designed to help users make informed decisions about existing rewards—not encourage unnecessary spending to earn more points.

### 1.1 Core Value Proposition

RedeemTrip helps users answer:

- Which flights match my travel requirements?
- Can I use my existing reward points for this trip?
- Is redeeming points better value than paying cash?
- Which verified transfer path, if any, could make my points usable?
- If I am short of points, how long might it take to accumulate the difference?
- What information should I verify before transferring or purchasing points?

### 1.2 Product Principles

1. **Data before AI:** Flight prices, award availability, program rules, balances, and calculations must come from verified data or user-confirmed inputs.
2. **Cash and award searches are distinct:** A cash fare is never represented as an award redemption.
3. **Deterministic calculations:** Financial comparisons and point calculations are performed by backend application logic, not generated by an LLM.
4. **Responsible rewards use:** Never encourage overspending, borrowing, or paying interest just to earn points.
5. **Transparent uncertainty:** Missing data and unsupported integrations must be disclosed.
6. **Booking is optional:** The core MVP is complete when it can search, compare, and explain results without booking a flight.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Travelers often have difficulty determining whether to pay for a flight or redeem reward points. Relevant information may be distributed across airlines, loyalty programs, credit cards, and rewards-search tools.

Users need to understand:

- Cash fares and award availability.
- Points required, taxes, and additional fees.
- The practical value of a redemption.
- Whether their existing programs can support a redemption.
- Whether a transfer is eligible and appropriate.
- The shortfall between their current balance and the required points.

RedeemTrip consolidates this decision-making process into a conversational travel-planning experience.

---

## 3. Target Users

### Primary Users

Travelers who possess airline miles, credit-card rewards, transferable bank points, or other eligible rewards and want to reduce the effective cost of travel.

### Secondary Users

Travelers who are learning how rewards work and need clear explanations of cash fares, redemption value, transfer restrictions, and points shortfalls.

---

## 4. MVP Goals and Non-Goals

### 4.1 Goals

- Provide a conversational trip-planning workflow.
- Collect structured travel requirements.
- Record the user's supported rewards programs and confirmed balances.
- Search cash-priced flight offers through a verified provider integration.
- Search award availability through a separate, verified integration where available.
- Compare comparable cash and award itineraries.
- Calculate redemption value and points shortfalls deterministically.
- Provide a basic estimate of how long a points shortfall might take to accumulate.
- Explain verified transfer paths and relevant restrictions.
- Follow responsible-use constraints when explaining recommendations.
- Provide useful error and fallback states when provider data is unavailable.

### 4.2 Non-Goals for the MVP

- Completing flight bookings or payments.
- Initiating airline-point transfers.
- Purchasing points on behalf of users.
- Recommending new credit-card applications.
- Personalized card-earning optimization.
- Personalized Revolut plan recommendations.
- Comprehensive shopping-portal and merchant-multiplier optimization.
- Guaranteed award availability or guaranteed redemption savings.
- Automatically moving balances between loyalty programs.

---

## 5. Core User Journey

1. The user signs in.
2. The user opens the trip-planning chat.
3. The user describes the intended trip in natural language.
4. The AI identifies missing requirements and asks follow-up questions.
5. The application collects the origin, destination, travel dates, passenger count, cabin, and date flexibility.
6. The application collects supported rewards programs, card or product details where relevant, and user-confirmed point balances.
7. The backend validates the collected information.
8. The application searches cash fares and, if an authorized award-search integration is available, award availability.
9. The backend normalizes the results and compares comparable itineraries.
10. The application calculates estimated redemption value, point shortfalls, and an accumulation timeline where sufficient user-provided data exists.
11. The AI explains the results using backend-provided facts.
12. The user can refine the trip, compare alternatives, or review relevant transfer guidance.
13. Before acting on transfer or points-purchase information, the user is shown the relevant conditions and limitations.

The user must not be shown fabricated live flight offers, award inventory, transfer eligibility, or purchase prices.

## 6. Functional Requirements

Priority definitions:

- **P0:** Required for the core MVP.
- **P1:** Important enhancement within the MVP where time and provider capabilities permit.
- **P2:** Future scope or optional extension.

### 6.1 Authentication and User Data — P0

- Users can sign up, sign in, and sign out.
- Protected endpoints authenticate users server-side.
- User-specific trip plans and rewards information are isolated by authenticated ownership.
- The application does not trust a user ID supplied by the client to authorize access.
- Rewards balances are user-confirmed inputs unless a verified integration explicitly provides them.
- The application must clearly distinguish stored user-provided information from provider-verified information.

### 6.2 Conversational Trip Planning — P0

- Users can describe their intended trip using natural language.
- The assistant identifies trip requirements and missing fields.
- The assistant asks relevant follow-up questions before initiating a search.
- The application maintains structured trip state across conversation turns.
- The user can correct previously provided information.
- The assistant can explain why additional information is needed.
- Invalid or ambiguous inputs are clarified instead of silently interpreted as confirmed facts.

Required trip information:

- Origin.
- Destination.
- Departure date.
- Return date for a return trip.
- One-way or return-trip preference.
- Passenger count.
- Cabin class.
- Date flexibility, if applicable.

Other information, such as nonstop preference, baggage needs, or preferred airlines, may be collected when relevant and supported.

### 6.3 Rewards Information — P0

The application collects the user's relevant rewards information, including:

- Rewards program or points currency.
- Associated card or financial product, where relevant.
- User-confirmed points balance.
- The currency or unit in which the balance is expressed.
- An estimated monthly points-earning amount, when the user wants a shortfall forecast and can provide a reasonable estimate.

The application must not infer a balance or earning rate from a card name.

The initial program list to investigate includes:

- Iberia.
- British Airways.
- Qatar Airways Privilege Club.
- Revolut.
- American Express Business Platinum.

This list is a starting point for program support, not a claim that every listed product or program supports transfers to every other program. Availability and eligibility may depend on the country, product, membership type, and current program terms.

### 6.4 Cash Flight Search — P0

- Integrate Duffel for cash-priced flight search, subject to API access, configuration, and provider capability verification.
- Validate search requests before sending them to the provider.
- Normalize provider responses into an application-owned flight-offer format.
- Display available itinerary details, including relevant dates, times, stops, cabin, and total price when provided by the source.
- Identify the currency and relevant fare conditions.
- Handle provider errors, timeouts, empty results, and incomplete offers.
- Never fabricate flight offers or prices.

Duffel is the intended cash-search integration. Its availability does not imply that it supplies airline award inventory.

### 6.5 Award-Flight Availability — P0 Requirement, Conditional on Integration

Award availability is a core product requirement, but its implementation depends on identifying and obtaining access to a suitable provider or authorized integration.

- Investigate award-search providers and verify API access, terms, pricing, airline coverage, and data limitations.
- Search award inventory only through an authorized integration or another explicitly verified data source.
- Normalize award results separately from cash offers.
- Display the points required, taxes, fees, program, and itinerary when supplied by verified data.
- Distinguish verified live availability from stale, incomplete, or unverified information.
- Never present cash offers as award results.
- Never invent point requirements, award inventory, or award taxes.
- If award search is unavailable, tell the user that award availability could not be verified and continue to provide cash-search results where possible.

A recommendation based on cash fares alone must be labeled as a cash-fare result, not a confirmed points-redemption recommendation.

### 6.6 Flight and Redemption Comparison — P0

The application compares comparable cash and award itineraries.

Comparison inputs may include:

- Comparable cash fare.
- Points required.
- Award taxes and fees.
- Currency.
- Itinerary details and travel dates.
- User-confirmed available points.
- Verified program restrictions where available.

#### Estimated Redemption Value

When the necessary values are available and the itineraries are sufficiently comparable:

`Estimated value per point = (Comparable cash fare − award taxes and fees) / points required`

The application must:

- Validate that the points-required value is greater than zero.
- Use consistent currencies or a verified currency conversion.
- Display the currency and points unit.
- Avoid calculations when required inputs are missing or incomparable.
- Explain that the result is an estimate and not a guarantee of optimal value.
- Consider relevant restrictions and opportunity cost in the explanation.
- Never let Groq invent or override the calculation.

The application may rank comparable options using explicit criteria such as estimated out-of-pocket cost and redemption value. The ranking method must be implemented in deterministic backend logic and its assumptions should be understandable to the user.

### 6.7 Points Shortfall — P0

For a confirmed award option:

`Points shortfall = max(0, points required − user-confirmed eligible balance)`

Requirements:

- Only use a balance that belongs to the relevant program or a verified eligible combination of programs.
- Do not automatically combine balances from unrelated programs.
- Distinguish points already available from points that might be transferred, purchased, or earned in the future.
- Explain when the user has enough points, a shortfall, or insufficient data to calculate a shortfall.
- Do not imply that a transfer will resolve the shortfall unless the path and eligibility have been verified.

### 6.8 Basic Points-Accumulation Timeline — P0

When the user has a points shortfall and supplies or confirms an estimated monthly points-earning amount, the application calculates an indicative accumulation timeline.

`Estimated months = points shortfall / estimated points earned per month`

Requirements:

- Use the user's confirmed estimate, not a fabricated card or program earning rate.
- Validate the shortfall and earning estimate.
- If the shortfall is zero, report that no additional points are needed for that option.
- If the estimate is missing, zero, negative, or unreliable, do not calculate a timeline; explain what information is needed.
- Clearly label any result as an estimate.
- Explain that actual accumulation may vary.
- Never encourage additional spending, debt, or interest-bearing borrowing to accelerate accumulation.

Detailed earning optimization remains out of scope.

### 6.9 Transfer-Path Guidance — P0

The application provides conditional, evidence-based information about transferring rewards between supported programs.

The initial investigation includes the documented BA/Avios-related paths involving:

- British Airways.
- Iberia.
- Vueling.
- Aer Lingus.
- Finnair.
- Qatar Airways Privilege Club.

Requirements:

- Maintain a verified list of supported programs and transfer relationships.
- Verify the direction and eligibility of each transfer path.
- Distinguish direct transfers from bridge-account or other program-connectivity arrangements.
- Verify the applicable account-age requirements, identity or passport-detail matching requirements, restrictions, and current terms for each specific path before displaying them as facts.
- Verify whether a financial product can transfer points into a particular program before suggesting the path.
- Do not assume that all Avios programs have identical rules or that all listed products can transfer to every listed program.
- Explain applicable fees, transfer ratios, processing times, and restrictions only when reliable information is available.
- Recommend considering a transfer only after a suitable redemption and its availability have been confirmed.
- Never initiate a transfer in the MVP.
- If information is not verified, clearly state that the transfer path or requirement needs confirmation.

### 6.10 Buying-Points Guidance — Informational MVP Behavior

The application may provide informational guidance about buying points when:

- A specific redemption has been identified.
- Required points and the user's eligible balance are known.
- The shortfall is relatively small.
- Reliable purchase prices and relevant redemption costs are available.

Where verified data is available, compare the cost of purchasing the missing points against the estimated savings of the redemption, taking taxes, fees, restrictions, and uncertainty into account.

Requirements:

- Do not purchase points on behalf of the user.
- Do not invent prices, bonuses, promotions, or purchase availability.
- Do not present a definitive recommendation when important costs are unknown.
- Clearly distinguish informational guidance from a completed transaction.
- Never encourage borrowing or unnecessary spending to buy points.

This feature may display a clear limitation or general informational guidance when reliable purchase data is unavailable.

### 6.11 Flexible-Date Search — P1

Flexible-date search originates from the original product brief.

- Allow the user to indicate flexibility around travel dates.
- Where supported by the provider, compare suitable dates within the requested window.
- Preserve passenger, cabin, route, and other relevant requirements.
- If the provider does not support flexible-date search, allow separate searches for selected dates where feasible.
- Clearly explain unsupported search capabilities.
- Do not invent dates, offers, or availability.

### 6.12 AI Explanations and Guardrails — P0

Groq is used for:

- Extracting structured trip intent from conversational messages.
- Identifying missing information.
- Asking relevant follow-up questions.
- Explaining backend-provided flight comparisons.
- Summarizing verified transfer conditions and known limitations.
- Helping users refine their search.

Groq must not be the authoritative source for:

- Flight prices or inventory.
- Award availability.
- Points balances.
- Provider coverage or capabilities.
- Program transfer rules.
- Purchase prices.
- Redemption calculations.
- Guaranteed savings or earning timelines.

The backend must validate inputs, retrieve data, enforce rules, and perform calculations.

#### Responsible-Use Constraints

AI explanations must:

- Never encourage unnecessary purchases or spending to earn points.
- Never encourage taking on debt or paying interest for rewards.
- Encourage rewards earned through spending the user already planned.
- Disclose uncertainty, relevant fees, and material restrictions.
- Avoid presenting points as free money.
- Avoid guaranteeing that a redemption is the best option.
- Recommend verifying time-sensitive program conditions before a transfer or purchase.
- Never invent provider data to make an answer appear complete.

### 6.13 Trip Plans and Persistence — P1

Where implemented within MVP time:

- Users can save and retrieve their trip searches or plans.
- Saved data is associated with the authenticated user.
- Users can revisit their trip requirements and results.
- Time-sensitive prices and award availability are clearly identified as requiring a fresh search or verification.
- The application does not imply that a previously displayed fare or award seat remains available.

### 6.14 Booking — P2, Optional Future Scope

Booking is not required for the core MVP.

Future booking functionality may include:

- Presenting a selected offer for booking.
- Integrating an authorized booking provider.
- Confirming the final price and fare conditions.
- Collecting required traveler details securely.
- Handling booking errors and payment status.
- Showing booking confirmation only after the provider confirms it.

Award booking, points transfers, and payment processing require separate verification of provider capabilities, terms, and security requirements.

The application must never claim a booking is confirmed unless the booking provider has confirmed it.

## 7. Non-Functional Requirements

### 7.1 Security

- Authenticate protected requests server-side.
- Enforce user ownership on all user-specific records.
- Keep provider credentials and secret keys on the server.
- Validate external input and provider responses.
- Avoid logging secrets, credentials, sensitive traveler details, or unnecessary personal data.
- Return safe error messages without exposing stack traces or internal secrets.

### 7.2 Reliability

- Handle provider timeouts, rate limits, invalid responses, and service failures.
- Distinguish no results from provider failure.
- Use explicit loading, empty, unsupported, and error states.
- Do not silently replace failed provider data with invented results.

### 7.3 Maintainability

- Use TypeScript and Zod validation.
- Keep provider integrations behind adapter boundaries.
- Separate conversational orchestration, provider access, normalization, and deterministic calculation logic.
- Keep cash-search and award-search adapters separate.
- Use backend Route Handlers for backend operations; do not introduce Server Actions.
- Preserve the existing Next.js, Supabase, Prisma, and project conventions.

### 7.4 Performance

- Avoid unnecessary repeated provider requests.
- Use appropriate timeouts and bounded request handling.
- Keep conversational responses and result rendering responsive.
- Apply caching only where appropriate for the data's freshness requirements. Do not treat cached availability or prices as guaranteed current data.

### 7.5 Transparency

- Identify the source and currency of displayed prices when available.
- Distinguish provider-verified facts from user-provided information and estimates.
- Disclose unsupported award search, flexible-date search, or transfer information.
- Do not promise savings, availability, or points accumulation.

## 8. High-Level Technical Architecture

### Frontend

- Next.js App Router.
- TypeScript.
- Tailwind CSS and the existing component system.
- Conversational trip-planning interface.
- Structured trip and rewards input.
- Flight results, comparison, and shortfall presentation.

### Backend

- Next.js Route Handlers.
- Supabase Auth for authentication.
- Prisma for server-side database access.
- Zod for request and response validation.
- Groq API for conversational intent extraction and explanations.
- Duffel adapter for cash-priced flight search, subject to verification.
- Separate award-search adapter only after authorized provider access is confirmed.
- Deterministic backend services for comparison, shortfall, and accumulation estimates.

### Logical Flow

1. The client submits a request to a Route Handler.
2. The handler authenticates the user when required and validates input.
3. Conversational orchestration updates structured trip state.
4. Provider adapters retrieve supported flight data.
5. Normalization and validation produce application-owned result structures.
6. Deterministic services calculate comparisons and points shortfalls.
7. Groq may explain the resulting structured facts.
8. The handler returns a validated response to the client.

No LLM-generated value may replace a missing live provider result or a deterministic calculation.

## 9. Assumptions and Dependencies

- Supabase Auth is the authentication provider.
- Prisma and the existing PostgreSQL database are used for application persistence.
- Groq provides conversational AI capabilities.
- Duffel is the intended cash-flight provider, subject to valid access and supported search capabilities.
- Award search requires a separately verified and authorized source.
- Program-transfer relationships and requirements must be checked against reliable, current documentation before actionable guidance is shown.
- Point balances and monthly earning estimates may be user-provided.
- Booking and automated point transfers are not required to demonstrate the core MVP.

Provider availability, API access, commercial terms, and loyalty-program rules remain dependencies until verified.

## 10. Acceptance Criteria

The MVP is acceptable when:

1. A user can authenticate and access their own trip-planning experience.
2. A user can describe a trip and answer follow-up questions.
3. The application collects and validates the required trip fields before searching.
4. The user can provide rewards programs and confirmed point balances.
5. The application returns cash-flight results from a functioning provider integration or clearly explains why search is unavailable.
6. Award results are displayed only when supplied by a verified, authorized award-search integration.
7. Cash fares and award offers are clearly distinguished.
8. The application does not invent live offers, award availability, point requirements, prices, transfer eligibility, or purchase prices.
9. Redemption calculations use validated, comparable values and deterministic backend logic.
10. Points shortfalls use the correct eligible balance and required points.
11. The accumulation timeline uses a user-confirmed monthly earning estimate and handles missing, zero, or invalid estimates safely.
12. Transfer guidance is conditional on verified program relationships and relevant eligibility rules.
13. Points-purchase guidance is informational and does not make unsupported claims.
14. AI explanations comply with responsible-use constraints.
15. Unsupported flexible-date and award-search capabilities produce clear fallback states.
16. Loading, empty, validation, timeout, and provider-error states are handled.
17. Protected data access is authenticated and authorized server-side.
18. Booking is not necessary for MVP completion.
